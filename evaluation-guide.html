Adaptive Dashboard Usability Evaluation Guide

Researcher setup: Open the dashboard, choose a scenario from Researcher setup, then click Enter participant view. Use Ctrl + Shift + M to restore researcher controls after the task.

Participant introduction

This is a simulated industrial device-management dashboard. You may select devices, inspect information, use filters, and perform available actions. The interface may reorganize or emphasize information according to the operational situation. Please work as you normally would and briefly explain what you are looking for and why. Some device processes are simulated and do not communicate with real equipment.

Practice task

Load Normal Monitoring. Ask the participant to select any normal device and inspect its details. This familiarizes them with device selection and action controls. Do not train them on any evaluated task.

Do not tell participants the target device name, the correct button, or the required action sequence. Give the scenario goal only. Provide help only when the participant is genuinely blocked, and record that assistance.

Task 1 — Critical Incident Handling

Load: Critical Incident

Participant instruction

A communication incident has occurred in the device environment. Use the dashboard to identify the affected device, investigate the problem, and restore communication if the available information indicates that recovery is possible. Tell me when you believe the system has returned to a stable state.

Expected successful behavior

  • Identifies and selects Gateway B2.
  • Recognizes that the device is disconnected and the incident is unresolved.
  • Runs connection diagnostics and interprets the result.
  • Attempts reconnection.
  • Recognizes the online/resolved state and cleared critical alert.

Observe

Time to identify incident; unnecessary device selections; understanding of diagnosis; action errors; recognition of resolution.

Task 2 — Firmware Update Supervision

Load: Firmware Supervision

Participant instruction

A firmware deployment is active while another operational issue remains in the fleet. Supervise the update through completion, verify its result, and confirm whether important information about the other operational issue remains accessible.

Expected successful behavior

  • Finds and selects Control Unit F6.
  • Understands the current and target firmware state.
  • Starts live progress monitoring once and waits for completion.
  • Verifies the completed installation.
  • Finds the critical Gateway B2 issue through alerts, device selection, or secondary information.

Observe

Ability to maintain task focus; understanding of progress/completion/verification; awareness of secondary critical information.

Task 3 — Multi-Device Prioritization

Load: Multi-Device Monitoring

Participant instruction

Several devices have different operational states. Determine which device requires immediate attention, focus the device list on critical devices, inspect the urgent device, and then restore the complete fleet overview.

Expected successful behavior

  • Identifies the critical category and Gateway B2.
  • Uses the Critical filter.
  • Inspects the critical device.
  • Returns the filter to All states and recognizes the complete overview.

Observe

Scanning strategy; filter discoverability; understanding of grouped priority; ability to recover the full overview.

Task 4 — Maintenance Planning

Load: Maintenance Planning

Participant instruction

A device requires preventive maintenance planning. Identify the device, review the maintenance requirement and prerequisites, and plan the inspection when the necessary information has been confirmed.

Expected successful behavior

  • Finds and selects Energy Meter E5.
  • Reviews the maintenance plan.
  • Completes all planning prerequisites.
  • Plans the inspection.
  • Recognizes the updated Inspection planned state.

Observe

Identification of maintenance state; checklist understanding; action sequencing; confirmation of the final planned state.

After each task

Collect NASA-TLX ratings and short comments. Suggested prompts:

  • What information helped you most?
  • Was anything unclear or unexpectedly emphasized?
  • Could you still access information outside the immediate task?

Final interview

  • Which context was easiest and which was most difficult?
  • Did the dashboard guide attention without taking away control?
  • Were any interface changes confusing?
  • What should be improved before this dashboard could be used in a real device-management environment?
